# Who Prayed Where, 1517–1789: Correcting the Religion Map

**Summary.** This audit corrects 31 errors in the religion map for 1517–1789, most visibly an Orthodox Anatolia that was Sunni. It shows how Ottoman rule kept Christian majorities under a Muslim state.

The map colours each place by the faith of most of its people, not of its ruler. That is why India shows Hinduism under the Mughals. By the same rule, Anatolia was Sunni Muslim, Ethiopia was Orthodox Tewahedo, Oman was Ibadi, Sri Lanka was Theravada Buddhist, and Joseon Korea was run on Neo-Confucian lines. Each era stands for the span until the next one: 1517–1699, 1700–1788 and 1789–1916.

## Albania converted late, and for political reasons

Sixteenth-century Ottoman tax registers show the Albanian districts overwhelmingly Christian, mostly under 5% Muslim. Elbasan was the highest at 5.5%, when Bosnia was already at 46%.³ The state pushed conversion from 1570. Reprisals after the papally backed rising of 1594–96 and the wars that followed broke the Catholic north: in the diocese of Pult, Catholics fell from 20,000 to 4,045 between the 1630s and 1670s.³ The Orthodox south held out longer and converted in waves after the Orlov revolt of 1770, when the Porte began to treat Orthodox subjects as Russia's fifth column.³ ⁴ In 1703 a papal synod denied communion to crypto-Catholics who passed as Muslims in public. Rome understood that many conversions were tactical.³ How much of Albania was Muslim by 1700 is still debated, so the map leaves that era unchanged for now.

## The millet system

The Porte governed non-Muslims through their own hierarchies. The Orthodox (Rum) millet answered to the Ecumenical Patriarch, and Armenians and Jews had their own.⁵ These hierarchies collected taxes, ran courts in personal law and answered for their flocks. Christians paid the cizye poll tax, and through the devşirme the state took Christian boys to staff the Janissaries and the bureaucracy.⁶ ⁷ A map coloured by population therefore shows Christian Balkans under a Muslim state. That is the correct picture, and it is why an Orthodox Anatolia is a plain error.

## Bosnia: the counter-case

Islam spread fast in Bosnia. The Bosnian Church was weak and squeezed between Rome and Orthodoxy, and conversion secured landholding rights under the timar system.⁸ Sixteenth-century registers already show Bosnia at 46% Muslim and Sarajevo at 100%.³

## Kosovo was not Muslim in 1517

The Serbian Patriarchate of Peć, restored in 1557, anchored Orthodoxy in Kosovo. Muslims reached a local majority in Opojë only by 1591. Wider conversion followed the Great Migration of Serbs in 1690 and the abolition of the Patriarchate in 1766.⁹ The map now shows Kosovo as Orthodox in 1517.
